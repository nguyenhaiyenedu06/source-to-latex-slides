# Evidence and independent review

## Source map

Maintain `evidence-map.md` in the output project. Record each substantive claim or visual:

| Slide/element | Claim or visual | Citation key | Exact source location | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Slide identifier and element | Statement, equation, number, example, or figure | Bibliography key | Printed page and PDF index if different; section, equation, table/figure, or stable heading | Qualifications, transformations, original versus adapted |

If a provided review reports another paper's result, attribute it as reported in that review unless the underlying paper is also available. Never imply inspection of an unavailable original.

Place citations near supported statements or captions. A shared citation may cover a clearly grouped block only when it supports every claim there. Include legible full references. Do not invent DOI, authors, dates, pages, or figure identifiers.

## Accessible examples

Use concrete examples or analogies understandable to high-school students. Cite the provided source supporting the principle. Label invented teaching examples as hypothetical/illustrative; cite their scientific basis, not a fictitious source for the scenario. Distinguish made-up numbers from measured results. Analogies must not introduce unsupported scientific knowledge, equivalence, or a misleading mechanism. Choose another example or report a gap if the sources cannot support its explanation.

## Visual evidence

Keep original figures alongside crops. Record original filename, source page/figure, and crop details. Label adapted/redrawn figures. For plotted data, retain original data and plotting code, record filtering/normalization/aggregation, and verify values, axes, uncertainty, units, and legends against the original. Disclose simplifications removing meaningful detail.

## Independent review contract

Give an independent agent this task:

> Audit the supplied presentation against provided original sources. Do not trust its bibliography or evidence map without checking originals. Check every substantive claim, number, equation, example, caption, figure, and code-drawn diagram. Verify whether each citation entails the nearby content and preserves scope, conditions, units, uncertainty, and attribution. Verify source figure identity and redrawn-chart data. Flag omissions, overstatements, incorrect references, and unsupported claims. Return slide/element, issue, exact supporting or contradicting source location, severity, and recommended correction. Report inaccessible sources as unverified. Source titles or abstracts alone do not establish claims requiring full text.

Require actionable findings or an explicit no-material-findings result with scope and access limits. Fix findings, remove unsupported claims, or ask for missing evidence. Re-review changed claims and affected conclusions. Record resolved issues and remaining limits in `review-notes.md`. A relevant source is not necessarily a supporting source.
